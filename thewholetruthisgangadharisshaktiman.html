The three bets I'd fund this quarter

Not thirteen findings — three decisions. Each with the evidence, the effort, and honest sizing logic. Everything else lives in the appendix.

1

The stock-out problem is bigger than any website problem.

This started as a website audit and surfaced a likely supply issue worth verifying fast. Reading the full live catalogue feed: roughly a third of all ~150 variants are marked OutOfStock. Not long-tail SKUs — whole lines. Every muesli variant reads as dark. The flavoured protein powders — mango, strawberry, pista badaam, ragi, both plant-protein cocoa variants — are almost entirely out of stock. On the Next Gen launch category, 4 of ~15 products show "Notify me" — and that one I confirmed live on the page, not just in the feed.

Muesli still holds a homepage category tile and a footer link — so we may be routing paid and organic traffic onto empty shelves. And every "Notify me" click is demand data that should already be flowing into the forecasting model.

Verify before acting: the feed header is current (30 Aug 2026) but some individual rows — muesli among them — carry older timestamps, so the availability flags need a 10-minute check against live inventory before we treat this as fact. If it holds, the sizing is stark: (assumptions labelled) if muesli + flavoured powders historically contributed even 10–15% of D2C revenue, that share is currently earning near-zero, making this plausibly the single largest revenue item in this memo — and no CRO experiment competes with restocking it. Ask: a same-day inventory check, a "Notify me" volume report by SKU (the demand signal already exists), and a rule that fully-dark categories get demoted from nav until stocked.
2

Fix the shareability layer — days of work, referral channel at stake.

Verified across multiple PDPs: product links shared on WhatsApp, Instagram DMs or X render with no preview image (og:image resolves to a literal /null) and a generic "Order online… delivery within 2-7 days" description identical on every product. Root cause is confirmed as dozens of null image_url records on migrated SKUs plus a meta template with no fallback. Two dead primary-nav links ("B.Y.O.B.", "New Launches" → #) belong in the same fix batch.

Sizing logic (illustrative, assumptions labelled): for a brand whose stated growth engine is word-of-mouth over paid social, shared links are a primary PDP entry path. Published link-preview research consistently shows rich previews lifting click-through 20–40% vs bare links. If shared links drive even 8% of PDP sessions, restoring previews is worth roughly a 2–3% lift in PDP traffic at zero media cost — recurring, forever. At any realistic D2C revenue base, that pays for the fix within the first day. Treat the percentages as hypotheses; the direction and the cost-benefit asymmetry are not in doubt. Ask: one sprint: image backfill, meta fallback, per-SKU title/description templates, nav links repaired.
3

Finish the migration — one punch-list, three payoffs.

The evidence says we're mid-way between platforms: old numeric SKU ids coexist with new variant_ ids, homepage assets still load from the previous vendor's CDN, footer links route via the legacy subdomain, and product pages ship with almost no server-rendered body — no name, price, ingredients or reviews in the HTML a crawler (or a slow Android connection) receives first. None of these is individually urgent since canonicals are set correctly; together they're a tax on every future initiative.

Why one bet, not three: SSR/ISR for PDPs with Product schema, catalogue data hygiene (categories, descriptions, images), and legacy cleanup share the same root — completing the platform move. Done together, we get product rich results in Google (stars + price, currently forfeited to marketplaces reselling us), faster first paint on mid-range Android (our majority device class), and an llmFeed that actually makes us the most machine-readable food brand in India — a claim worth owning as commerce shifts to AI agents. Two questions before I touch anything

Two things I could call defects — but the right answer depends on intent I don't have, and guessing would send the roadmap the wrong way.

1. "Shop" in the header routes to the Next Gen kids' category. Deliberate bet, or leftover?

If it's a conscious launch-quarter decision to point the whole store at Next Gen, I'll support it with better cross-sell into the adult range and we measure the trade-off explicitly. If it's a default nobody revisited, the majority of intent (protein powders and bars — our ₹2,799–4,499 highest-ticket SKUs) is paying an extra hop, and I'd A/B "Shop → all categories" within a week. Either way it becomes a measured choice instead of an accident.

2. Is there already a migration punch-list, and who owns it?

If the Dukaan remnants, subdomain footer links, null image records and empty catalogue fields are on a tracked list, Bet 3 is mostly prioritisation and I'll fold into the existing plan. If there's no list, that's the real finding — the migration is "done" in the way a half-labelled pack is done, and it needs an owner and an end date.

Instrumentation & method

Before any experiment ships: full-funnel events (view → ATC → checkout → payment success/failure by method), Core Web Vitals RUM segmented by device tier, payment-failure recovery tracking (UPI failure recovery alone typically recovers 1–2% of revenue in Indian D2C), and a weekly "Notify me" demand report by SKU.

Method: direct inspection of served HTML and metadata on the homepage, two category pages, two PDPs, the shop subdomain, and the complete llmFeed.json catalogue (~150 variant rows, feed dated 30 Aug 2026), on 31 Aug 2026. Every defect cited was observed in live responses. What outside-in couldn't see: checkout internals (client-rendered, requires session), real conversion rates, RUM performance, payment failure rates, and cohort behaviour — exactly what the instrumentation above unlocks. All revenue sizing is explicitly labelled as hypothesis with stated assumptions; the direction of each bet does not depend on the exact numbers.
