What's working

  • Reimbursement kills the hardest physician fear ✅ — "get 10 paying patients" becomes "a revenue line you can bill," language US physicians already understand.
  • "Built to be billed" is smart positioning ✅ — using engagement data as billing documentation turns the app's logs into money for the practice.
  • Positioning against incumbents is sharp ✅ — physician-led, vs consumer apps and vendors that bypass the physician.

Worries & problems to solve 😭❗️

  • For the reimbursement route — it is critical to get outcomes
    • "Workflow hiccups with the medical staff" are slowing the one pilot that matters.
    • Primary's defense against Cadence and HealthSnap is "our physician-led programs produce better outcomes."
    • Without the prep-score result, the pitch reduces to "another RPM/CCM vendor, with a smaller take."
  • Mismatch on the device codes that reimburse vs the programs we run — the programs that prove outcomes and the programs that bill cleanly aren't the same list yet.

Insights

The wedge

Compliant by design

A July 2026 proposed rule would require RPM/RTM clinical staff to be direct employees of the billing practice — ending the outsourced-nurse model that Cadence / HealthSnap-style vendors use. It does not restrict buying software.

So Primary can own a line nobody else can: "software that makes your own staff run chronic care for far more patients — billable, documented, audit-ready." A regulatory headwind becomes a moat.

Say "proposed." The final rule isn't out yet (usually early Nov), and it also proposes to cut some RPM/RTM rates — so don't over-anchor the per-patient number.
Learn from it

Talk to the Gradia Health founder

GI clinics, between-visit care, nurse and dietitian teams → founded 2021, now not operational. Founder Rithvik Seela — had raised $4.2M in 2023, now sunsetted. Worth a direct conversation on why; the likely lesson is the services-margin trap, which is exactly what Primary's software-only model must avoid.

On fundraising

  • Curious on the delays — with the current team and effort, why is fundraising this hard and slow?
    • Hypothesis: pitching a seed round with a Series-A-sized story but pre-seed evidence, in a crowded category, on a billing pathway regulators are actively tightening.
      • Traction is thin where it matters:
      • ~6 program patients per physician (121 ÷ 21).
      • Outcomes are anecdotal (one −23 lbs case).
      • The headline "16×" engagement stat is selection bias.
      • That's ~2 years in, which VCs read as slow.

What an a16z partner would say is missing — assuming the deck answers these:

  • 1. Where's revenue? Users, physicians and streaks, but no ARR and no evidence anyone pays. Has a single claim been submitted and paid? One paid Medicare claim beats a $12.1B TAM slide.
  • 2. Show me unit economics. Licensing fee per physician? Physician CAC? Payback? Gross margin once someone does the required monthly minutes?
  • 3. 6 patients per physician isn't a business. How does a practice get to 50–100 enrolled, billable patients, and how fast?
  • 4. Why do you beat Cadence and HealthSnap? Both already bill these codes, with staff. The answer has to be outcomes, and there's no controlled outcome yet. The "16×" stat is selection bias and "−23 lbs" reads as one patient.
  • 5. Who does the billable work? If it's the practice, it's not "near-zero effort." If it's Primary, it's a services company with services margins.
  • 6. What's the payer mix? Current programs skew under-65 and commercial. The $75–225K-per-physician claim assumes a Medicare panel.
  • 7. Regulatory & compliance? FDA status for RTM and RPM; audit risk on auto-generated documentation. No billing, revenue-cycle or compliance expertise visible on the team.
  • 8. Why aren't you in ACCESS? It's the most on-thesis tailwind in the market, and it's absent from the page.

What to add to the deck

  • A new headline (the wedge): "Compliant by design — your own staff runs chronic care for more patients, billable, documented, audit-ready." (Phrase the rule as proposed.)
  • A "one real practice" box: patients enrolled → claims submitted / paid / denial % → staff minutes/patient → net $ to the practice → Primary's fee. One real practice beats any TAM slide.
  • A focus statement: "Medicare chronic care: cardio-metabolic + MSK." Name the 2–3 programs and the exact codes each bills.
  • An outcome on a billable program: one cohort metric with n and timeframe. Keep colonoscopy as a one-line credibility note, not the headline.
  • Density metrics: eligible → enrolled → active at day 30 → billed, per practice. Shift from "21 physicians" to "patients per practice."
  • Compliance as a feature: auto-documentation, consent capture, established-patient / initiating-visit checks, audit logs.
  • The program library as the moat: built once, reused across practices at near-zero marginal cost — the network-effect story that makes it venture-scale.
  • Milestone-based use of funds: "$2.5M takes us to ~35 practices, ~$1.2M ARR, default alive by month X."
