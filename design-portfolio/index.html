01 · Project Sakura 2025

I recovered from clinical depression.

It was challenging, and it changed me. Then a question stuck: why aren't these things taught to us? Self-compassion, self-esteem, how to respond when someone around you is struggling. The people around me didn't know how to respond, and so many people struggle silently.

The goal is simple: help someone struggling with tools and comfort.

For the people around them

How to respond when someone is struggling

1

Don't compare pain

“Others have it worse” makes people feel unheard. Acknowledge it, and listen.

2

Gratitude can't be imposed

It only comes from within. People in pain need understanding and hope, not guilt.

3

Add to hope

You don't need to fix them. Remind them: “This is temporary. You are not alone.”

The moment

40% left before playing a single word

1 2
  1. 140% of people left at “let's go”, the very first tap. We talked to users to find out why.
  2. 2The ones who got past it stayed, about 8 minutes a session. The game worked; the door didn't.

The fix: we cut login and username so people jump straight into a quiz. Drop-off fell from 40% to 10%.

BTS: the content pipeline and my checklistOpenClose+

Two people, daily releases. An LLM pipeline turned one word into quizzes, explanations and image prompts, with a human check before anything shipped.

My content checklist at the time: copy a fifth grader understands, every step low effort, a casual and friendly vibe.

What I'd change today: I'd write “busy”, not “lazy”, and design for real progress rather than dopamine hits.

Honest result: day-1 retention held at 25%, short of our 40% goal.

How the coach thinks

1 2 3 4

03 · 78Health · Primary app 2026

Physician-led programs, made easy to follow

Primary turns a physician's expertise into daily, AI-guided health programs that bridge the gap between clinic visits. It was sold as a proactive coach, but built like a tracker and a program store. I reviewed it, then rebuilt the core as a working prototype.

  • RoleSelf-initiated UX review and a working prototype
  • ResultThe team adopted the direction
  • ToolsFigma; HTML, CSS and JS with Claude Code

An empty store on day one, a wall of nutrient bars, and a tutorial for gestures.

After · my prototype · live
  • Today's plan first, not an empty store.
  • Tick a task and the ring fills.
  • My Day: one hero number and one symptom to log.
  • Ask Primary: short, kind replies, with typing dots while it thinks.
Feedback that matters, I shared

The first five minutes, annotated

drag sideways →

  1. 1If you have to teach the gesture, the UI has failed. No one reads this much on a first screen.
  2. 2New users land in an empty store. The doctor's link should be the default first path, and the copy should say “Join a program”.
  3. 3Fourteen nutrient bars. Show one hero number and what's next, not everything I logged.
  4. 4One screen, one headline, one action. Make text legible, with pointers and spacing.
  5. 5Chat felt slow. Check what kind of question it is before reasoning, and use typing dots so the wait feels instant.
Tone

Nudges need to be positive

Comparing today with yesterday reads as guilt, and guilt doesn't bring people back. Lead with what's possible next.

BTS: my full review, 23 points in four tiersOpenClose+

Fix first: what do I do today, kill the empty state, cut walls of text, one hero metric, short and fast chat.

Structural: one typeface and four text roles, one template for every AI output, no teaching gestures.

Polish: light theme with readable contrast, no unlabeled counters, “Free” instead of “Rs 0”.

Don't reinvent the wheel: one daily score like Oura, streaks like Duolingo, reminders over WhatsApp.

Keep: food-photo logging, the proactive nudges and doctor-authored programs.
